Health Insurance Experience Redesign

Redesigning the end-to-end health insurance purchase journey to improve conversion, reduce drop-offs, and simplify decision making for users buying policies for themselves and family members.

Role
Product Designer
Duration
3 weeks
Platform
Mobile Web + App
Scope
Discovery, UX, UI, Validation

01 Problem

Where the journey was broken

Confusing member selection

Users struggled with who-to-insure logic; complex family combinations created confusion and dead ends.

Form drop-offs

High abandonment during medical declaration and proposer detail forms due to long, unguided inputs.

Poor plan discovery

Filters and plan comparison lacked clarity, leaving users unable to make confident decisions.

Reactive error handling

Errors surfaced after submission rather than being prevented inline, causing frustration and re-entry.

02 Baseline

Where we started

18.6%Conversion rate
27%Drop-off at member selection
34%Drop-off at medical declaration
6.8 minAvg. time to purchase
22%Error rate in form sessions
9%Plan comparison usage

03 Goals

What we set out to achieve

  1. Increase overall conversion by reducing funnel drop-offs at every stage.
  2. Simplify family member selection for varied combination scenarios.
  3. Improve form completion through preventive error handling and inline guidance.
  4. Make plan filtering and comparison intuitive so users find the right plan faster.
  5. Reduce time to purchase by streamlining decision points across the journey.

04 Design

Eight areas of redesign

01

Member selection

Converted to modular chips with real-time logic and progressive disclosure for separate-policy cases.

02

Guided forms

Inline validation, contextual hints, smart defaults, and reduced redundant fields lowered error loops.

03

Family complexity

Covered real-world combinations through inline nudges and system-driven constraints instead of silent failures.

04

Plan discovery

Pre-applied filters and clearer bottom-sheet filtering helped users compare and decide faster.

05

Add-ons

Grouped optional upgrades with price tags and benefit summaries to reduce cognitive load.

06

Medical declaration

Started with a binary decision and expanded only when needed, making dense forms less intimidating.

07

Error handling

Moved from reactive errors to preventive field-level validation with clear, actionable messages.

08

Post-purchase

Created clearer confirmation states and a feedback moment to close the insight loop.

05 Impact

Results after redesign

+44%Conversion rate, 18.6% to 26.9%
-48%Drop-off at member selection, 27% to 14%
-44%Drop-off at medical declaration, 34% to 19%
-38%Time to purchase, 6.8 to 4.2 min
-59%Error rate in forms, 22% to 9%
+133%Plan comparison usage, 9% to 21%

06 Reflection

What this project reinforced

Reducing friction across the entire funnel and improving clarity in complex family scenarios led to faster decision making and strong uplift in conversion.

The biggest lesson: preventive design always outperforms reactive fixes. Catching errors before they happen through smart defaults, inline validation, and progressive disclosure had a compounding effect across every stage of the journey.

Colrows

From Idea to Product

Designing an AI-first data workspace that turns complex workflows into real-time decision systems anyone can use, not just analysts.

Role
Sole Freelance Product Designer
Platform
Web App
Focus
AI Chat, Querying, Data Viz
Scope
End-to-end UX/UI

01 Problem

Organizations do not lack data. They lack speed and clarity.

Data is siloed

Data lived across disconnected systems with no unified layer, making cross-source questions hard without analyst involvement.

Teams depended on analysts

Every business question became a request. Queries took hours or days, slowing decisions across the organization.

BI tools felt fragmented

Existing tools required SQL knowledge and context switching across platforms, creating a steep curve for non-technical users.

Decision latency was real

In one deployment, teams took 23 days to answer a single business question, directly hurting speed and confidence.

02 Goal

Make data access instant and self-serve

  1. Let anyone query data instantly without SQL or analyst dependency.
  2. Use AI to remove the barrier between a question and an answer.
  3. Make insights explainable, editable, and actionable instead of black-box outputs.
  4. Move decision-making from delayed requests to real-time exploration.

03 Approach

Five design decisions that shaped the product

01

One core action

The product centered around one simple prompt: ask a question. That gave new users a near-zero learning curve.

02

Structured AI outputs

Answers appeared as tables, charts, and editable SQL so users could verify what the AI returned.

03

Explainability first

Users could inspect sources, edit queries, and rerun outputs to build trust through control rather than faith.

04

Question to action

AI suggested visualizations automatically and kept exploration in one continuous workflow.

05

Multiple personas

Non-technical users got conversation, analysts got SQL control, and enterprises got role-based access.

04 Features

Key product surfaces designed

Conversational querying

Natural-language prompts for teams who needed answers without writing SQL.

Editable SQL

Generated queries stayed visible and adjustable for analysts and power users.

Real-time charts

Charts and visual summaries generated directly from the answer flow.

Unified workspace

A single place to ask, inspect, visualize, and continue exploring data.

05 Impact

From a live enterprise deployment

-90%+Decision latency, with questions answered in minutes instead of days
8xData adoption, with business teams querying data directly
-80%IT and data team requests shifted toward self-serve analytics
<4 minTime to answer a business question, down from 23 days

06 Before vs After

What changed

Before

23 days to answer a business question. Heavy dependency on analysts. Static dashboards and fragmented data sources.

After

Answers in under four minutes. Self-serve exploration for business teams. Real-time insights from a unified layer.

07 Reflection

What this project reinforced

AI products must prioritize trust and explainability above everything else. Good UX hides complexity without removing capability.

The outcome: Colrows evolved into a system where business teams could go from question to insight to action in minutes, without relying on data teams.

ReFi NFT Dashboard

Making climate investing accessible through NFTs

Designing a Web3 dashboard that makes climate-positive assets, staking, wallets, and NFT ownership easier to understand and act on.

Role
Product Designer
Platform
Web Dashboard
Focus
Web3, NFTs, Climate Finance
Scope
End-to-end UX/UI

01 Problem

Climate finance and Web3 both come with steep learning curves

Unfamiliar terminology

Users had to understand staking, wallets, token ownership, and environmental impact before they could make a confident decision.

Fragmented information

Financial performance, NFT details, and climate impact lived across separate surfaces without one clear story.

Low trust

Abstract claims and opaque blockchain activity made it difficult for users to verify where their money and impact were going.

Complex actions

Connecting a wallet and completing transactions introduced unfamiliar steps, states, and failure points.

02 Goal

Turn a complex investment system into a clear guided experience

  1. Explain every investment opportunity in plain language.
  2. Connect financial value with visible, measurable climate impact.
  3. Make wallet and transaction states clear before, during, and after every action.
  4. Give users one dashboard for assets, rewards, NFTs, and portfolio performance.

03 Design

Four decisions that shaped the dashboard

01

Impact-first discovery

Projects lead with a clear impact story, supported by financial information and asset details.

02

Progressive disclosure

Core information stays simple while advanced blockchain and transaction details remain available when needed.

03

Transparent ownership

NFT status, wallet activity, rewards, and project allocation are connected in one traceable view.

04

Guided transactions

Every transaction communicates the next step, network state, cost, confirmation, and recovery path.

04 Outcome

A more understandable bridge between investing and climate impact

One portfolio view

Users can understand holdings, rewards, NFT ownership, and environmental contribution without switching tools.

More confident decisions

Clear hierarchy and plain-language explanations reduce the knowledge required to explore an opportunity.

05 Reflection

What this project reinforced

Emerging technology becomes useful only when the interface makes its value, risks, and system state understandable. The dashboard treats transparency as part of the product experience, not supporting documentation.

Life Insurance

Life Insurance Experience Redesign

Redesigning the end-to-end life insurance journey to make a complex, long-term financial decision easier to understand, evaluate, and complete with confidence.

Role
Product Designer
Platform
Mobile Web + App
Focus
Trust, Conversion, Forms
Scope
Journey Design, UX, UI, Error States

01 Context

Life insurance asks for commitment before users feel confident

Buying life insurance is different from buying most financial products. Users are making a decision that may affect their family for decades while trying to understand coverage, policy duration, premiums, eligibility, health declarations, and payment.

The existing journey contained the information required to complete a purchase, but it often placed the burden of understanding that information on the user. The challenge was not simply to shorten the flow. It was to reduce uncertainty without removing information users genuinely needed to make a responsible decision.

02 Problem

Where confidence started breaking down

Too much insurance language

Users encountered product terminology before understanding what it meant for them, making basic decisions feel heavier than necessary.

Disconnected journey

Discovery, quotation, plan selection, proposal details, health declarations, and payment felt like separate stages instead of one continuous decision.

Unclear premium decisions

Coverage, tenure, payment frequency, and plan configuration affected premiums, but the relationship between those decisions was hard to follow.

Form fatigue

As users moved closer to purchase, the journey became form-heavy. Long sections and repeated questions made progress feel slower than it was.

Late errors

Some eligibility, validation, and data issues surfaced only after users had already invested significant effort.

Trust drop-offs

The highest-friction screens appeared exactly where users were sharing sensitive information or making the final financial commitment.

03 Challenge

Simplify the experience without oversimplifying the decision

Life insurance has unavoidable complexity. Coverage decisions have consequences, health information matters, regulatory information cannot disappear, and different customer and payment states introduce extra branches.

Instead of asking how to remove more steps, I approached the problem as: how can every step earn its place in the journey? The goal was to make complexity appear only when it became relevant and keep users informed about why each decision mattered.

04 Goals

What we set out to achieve

  1. Make coverage, premiums, and important plan decisions understandable before users commit.
  2. Create continuity from discovery through proposal, payment, and confirmation.
  3. Reduce cognitive load across long forms using progressive disclosure and better grouping.
  4. Prevent avoidable errors before submission instead of asking users to recover afterwards.
  5. Build trust around sensitive moments such as health declarations and payment.
  6. Support multiple customer and payment scenarios without making the primary journey feel complicated.

05 Design Principle

Explain, decide, confirm

Explain

Give context first

Give users enough information to understand what a decision means before asking them to act.

Decide

Focus the choice

Present one focused decision without unnecessary information competing for attention.

Confirm

Show what changed

Make the result of the choice clear, including what happens next in the journey.

Used across

High-trust moments

This principle shaped coverage selection, plan configuration, health declarations, and payment decisions.

06 Design

Eight areas that shaped the redesign

01

Easier starting point

The journey reduced the amount of insurance knowledge expected from users and began with the decision they were trying to make: protecting themselves and their family.

02

Coverage and premium connection

When users changed a plan parameter, the premium impact stayed visible so configuration felt like understanding trade-offs, not just entering values.

03

Transparent plan selection

Plans were structured around the details most useful for the next decision, while secondary details stayed accessible without overwhelming comparison.

04

Manageable forms

Related questions were grouped, sections became easier to scan, field guidance moved closer to the input, and unnecessary repetition was reduced.

05

Health declarations with context

Medical and good-health declarations were treated as high-trust decisions, with clearer language and confirmation around choices with significant consequences.

06

Earlier validation

Requirements and system constraints surfaced closer to the moment of input, changing error handling from recovery after failure to prevention before submission.

07

Explicit payment choices

Payment options such as full payment or EMI were shown as clear alternatives with amount and expectation visible before selection.

08

Continuity until completion

Selected plan, coverage, and premium stayed present through the funnel, and confirmation screens clarified what was purchased and what happens next.

07 Outcome

From a sequence of forms to one continuous decision

Clearer plan decisions

Coverage, premium, and important plan information became easier to evaluate together rather than across disconnected screens.

Lower perceived complexity

Progressive disclosure and better grouping made a long insurance journey feel more manageable without hiding required information.

Earlier error prevention

Validation and constraints moved closer to user input, reducing unnecessary recovery loops later in the journey.

Stronger continuity

Users retained context about what they were purchasing as they moved from discovery to proposal and payment.

Deliberate trust moments

Sensitive decisions around health information and payment received additional explanation and confirmation instead of being treated like ordinary form inputs.

08 What I learned

Trust is created by the journey, not added to the interface

This project reinforced that trust in financial products rarely comes from one reassurance message, illustration, or visual treatment. It comes from hundreds of smaller decisions: explaining something before asking for it, showing the effect of a choice immediately, preventing an error before it happens, keeping important information visible, and being explicit about what happens next.

Life insurance will always contain complexity. Good product design does not pretend that complexity does not exist. It introduces it at the moment the user is ready for it.

Go Leap

Immersive Product Discovery

A freelance project focused on turning static catalogue browsing into a richer, more guided product discovery experience.

Role
Freelance Product Designer
Platform
Web
Focus
Catalogue, Discovery, Visual UX
Scope
UX/UI Design

01 Focus

From passive browsing to active exploration

Better discovery

Restructured browsing so users could understand product categories and details without friction.

Visual clarity

Used stronger hierarchy and imagery to make the catalogue feel more tangible and easier to scan.

Guided movement

Created a flow that helped users move from overview to product-level detail naturally.

Conversion support

Reduced dead ends by making key actions and product information available at the right moments.

02 Reflection

What this project reinforced

Discovery experiences need rhythm: enough structure to compare, enough visual detail to feel real, and enough momentum to keep people moving.
